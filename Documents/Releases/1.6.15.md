iGhostVT 1.6.15 adds a drop zone to the relay, so a file moves between two devices with `ighostvt-cli drop` instead of being pasted through a terminal.

- Moving a file between devices meant encoding it as text and pasting it into a shell, which crawled. Every device that has the relay's configuration can now put files on the relay and take them from it: `ighostvt-cli drop put <file>` on one device, `ighostvt-cli drop` to list and `ighostvt-cli drop get <file>` on another. Files travel compressed with zstd and are checked on arrival, keep their permissions and modification time, and an upload or download that was cut off resumes when the same command runs again.
- The relay keeps a file for 30 minutes after the last device that put or got it disconnected, `ighostvt-cli drop rm` removes one sooner, and the relay forgets every file when it restarts. Drop zone files are not encrypted, unlike terminal sessions through the relay: keep secrets out of it. Update the relay's image to use it; `RELAY_DROP_BYTES` sets the space it may take (4 GiB by default) and `0` turns it off. The app itself has no drop zone controls, and devices on 1.6.14 keep working with this relay and with 1.6.15.

Choose `iphoneos-arm64e` for roothide, `iphoneos-arm64` for a rootless bootstrap, `iGhostVT-1.6.15-macos-notarized.zip` for a Mac — it is signed and notarized, so it opens without clearing quarantine, and updates itself — or `GhostRemote-1.6.15.ipa` for an iPhone or iPad without custom firmware, which your sideloading app signs as it installs it. To run the relay, use the attached `compose.yml`; the image is `ghcr.io/owngoal-dev/ighostvt-relay`, and the digest is in `relay-image.txt`. SHA-256 sums are in `SHA256SUMS`, and the Mac app's in `SHA256SUMS.macos`.
